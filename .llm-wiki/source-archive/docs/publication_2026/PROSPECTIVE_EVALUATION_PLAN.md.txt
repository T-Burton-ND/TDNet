# 2026 prospective evaluation plan

Central question: how does increasing data richness affect predictive
performance across models of increasing complexity, and how does that
relationship change as information accumulates during the season?

The frozen matrix is compact and balanced: the six prespecified architecture
representatives (M1, M2, M3, M4, M5, M10) are evaluated across F0–F6. F7 is
market-only and F8 is F6-plus-market; both are comparative-only. The operational poll roster
is separate and explicitly registered. Winner-objective models are preserved
for scientific record only and are excluded from routine official predictions.

The primary full-season endpoint is Brier score. Secondary endpoints are winner
accuracy, margin MAE, chalk recall, and upset recall. The same endpoints will
be reported in the fixed secondary information-maturity phases: PHASE_1 weeks
0–3, PHASE_2 weeks 4–6, PHASE_3 weeks 7–9, and PHASE_4 week 10 onward.
The prospective study ends with the FBS regular season. Report bucket sample sizes,
uncertainty intervals, missing predictions, and multiplicity handling.

Fingerprint × phase, model-family × phase, and changing market-aware advantage
by phase are prespecified secondary analyses. The three-way interaction and
team prior-games analysis are exploratory. Postseason analyses are outside the
2026 prospective protocol and must be labeled retrospective or exploratory.
